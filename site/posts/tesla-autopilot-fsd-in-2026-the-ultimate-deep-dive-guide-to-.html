Decoding the Tiers: Basic Autopilot vs. Enhanced vs. FSD Supervised

To understand Tesla’s autonomous ecosystem, you must first understand the distinction between its driver-assist tiers. Tesla does not treat every vehicle's autonomous suite equally; capabilities depend heavily on software packages unlocked by the owner.

1. Basic Autopilot (Standard on Every Tesla)

Every Tesla manufactured today comes standard with Basic Autopilot. This is essentially a highly refined Level 2 Advanced Driver Assistance System (ADAS). It combines two primary components:
* Traffic-Aware Cruise Control (TACC): Matches your speed to surrounding traffic, coming to a complete stop and resuming movement in stop-and-go highway conditions.
* Autosteer: Keeps the vehicle centered within its clearly marked lane using real-time vision processing.

Basic Autopilot is best suited for long, monotonous highway commutes where maintaining lane positioning and speed distance reduces driver fatigue.

2. Enhanced Autopilot (EAP)

Enhanced Autopilot adds key navigational conveniences tailored primarily for controlled-access highways and parking lots:
* Navigate on Autopilot: Automatically suggests and executes lane changes, takes highway interchanges, and handles off-ramps based on active navigation settings.
* Auto Lane Change: Safely transitions the car into adjacent lanes when the driver signals or when the car determines a lane change is necessary to maintain cruising speed.
* Autopark & Summon: Allows the car to self-park in parallel or perpendicular spots, and enables drivers to maneuver the car in or out of tight parking spaces remotely using the Tesla mobile app.

3. Full Self-Driving Supervised (FSD)

FSD is the crown jewel of Tesla’s AI development. It builds on Enhanced Autopilot by bringing autonomous navigation to city streets and complex urban environments.

FSD handles:
* Unprotected left and right turns across oncoming traffic.
* Multi-lane roundabouts and complex multi-way stop signs.
* Pedestrian, cyclist, and debris avoidance in dense urban sectors.
* Speed adjustments for school zones, traffic lights, and road construction zones.
