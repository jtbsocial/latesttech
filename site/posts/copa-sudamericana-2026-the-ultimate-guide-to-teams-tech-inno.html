The Copa Sudamericana 2026 maintains its high-octane format while introducing optimized match-scheduling algorithms designed to minimize team travel fatigue across the continent.

Phase 1: National Qualification

Clubs from Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay, and Venezuela compete in single-leg national phase matches to determine which two teams from each nation advance to the group stage.

Phase 2: The Group Stage

Argentina and Brazil enter directly at this phase with six teams each, joined by the 16 phase-one winners and four teams eliminated from the Copa Libertadores qualifying rounds. The 32 teams are divided into eight groups of four.

Knockout Play-offs & Final Journey

  • Group Winners: Automatically qualify for the Round of 16.
  • Runner-Up Play-offs: Group runners-up face third-place teams dropping down from the Copa Libertadores group stage in a two-legged playoff.
  • Single-Match Final: Played at a neutral venue selected for its modern infrastructure and capacity.

High-Tech Breakthroughs in the 2026 Edition

Modern sports broadcasting and officiating have taken a massive leap forward in 2026, and CONMEBOL is at the forefront of this digital transformation.

1. Semi-Automated Offside Technology (SAOT)

Following successful implementations in international competitions, SAOT makes its full-season debut in the Copa Sudamericana 2026. Utilizing 12 dedicated tracking cameras mounted beneath stadium roofs, the system tracks 29 data points per player, providing immediate 3D render visualizations to VAR officials within seconds.

2. Micro-Cam & AI Tactical Feeds

Select marquee matches feature player micro-cams integrated into jersey collars, giving home viewers a zero-distance perspective during goal celebrations and set pieces. Additionally, AI algorithms analyze real-time spatial positioning, generating dynamic heatmaps and pass probability metrics on official streaming feeds.

3. Ultra-Low Latency 4K HDR Streaming

With global viewership at an all-time high, primary rights holders have adopted modern AV1 encoding pipelines, delivering 4K HDR video at 60fps with less than four seconds of broadcast glass-to-glass latency.
